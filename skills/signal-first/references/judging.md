# Judging posts and accounts

Two scores, kept apart, plus flags. Write them down when you judge more than a couple of
posts; the user can disagree with a line, not with a vibe.

## Value (0-2 each, 10 max)

| question | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **First-hand?** | relays others, no source | reports with a source | did it, saw it, built it, measured it |
| **Specific?** | adjectives, "game-changer" | some names or numbers | numbers, versions, steps, code, screenshots of real things |
| **Checkable?** | nothing to check | a link or a name | data, repo, demo, or confirmed by others in replies |
| **New?** | restates the news or a known list | a new angle on known facts | facts or results not elsewhere yet |
| **Useful here?** | off the question | related | answers part of the question |

Read the replies before you score "checkable": a correction from the maintainer, a community
note or the author's own walk-back changes the score.

Rough reading: 8-10 cite it; 5-7 use it with a caveat; 0-4 do not rely on it.

## Reach (describe, don't score)

Views, likes, reposts, quotes; the `save`, `talk` and `spread` ratios; and for the account,
`breakout` (best post against the median). Write one line: "138k views, 1,100x the author's
median; saves almost equal likes". Reach explains why you are seeing a post. It is not evidence.

## Flags

Name each one you see, with the words that show it.

- **Engagement bait**: "comment 'AI' and I'll DM you the guide", "like if you agree", polls
  with no stakes, rage questions. A real question the author wants answered is not bait.
- **Recycled list**: "10 AI tools that will replace...", threads of other people's work, no
  credit, nothing the author tried.
- **Borrowed reach**: tagging large accounts who are not part of the story; follow-back rings
  ("互关", "follow for follow"); giveaway follow trains.
- **Outrage framing**: a real fact wrapped to make people angry; check the fact separately.
- **Uncredited screenshot**: someone else's chart or post, cropped, without a link.
- **AI slop**: generic claims, every paragraph the same length, symmetrical lists, a hook line
  plus three bullets plus a question, words like "delve", "game-changer", "Here's the thing",
  "让我们", "赋能", heavy em dashes in Chinese, and nothing a person had to be there to know.
  Judge by the missing specifics, not by one word. Real people use dashes too.
- **Sales in disguise**: a "lesson" that ends at a paid course or a referral link. Fine if
  declared; a flag if hidden.

A flag lowers trust, not necessarily value: a baity post can still carry one true number.
Extract the number, cite it, and say what the post was.

## Accounts

Run `x_audit`, then read the top posts **by saves**. Then answer:

1. What does this account know first-hand? (its work, its field, its data)
2. Do the originals carry value, or does the reach come from replies, reposts and one viral post?
3. Is the pattern stable? Same quality over months, not one week.
4. Any flags across posts (above)? One baity post is a lapse; a pattern is the account.

Verdicts for the sources file: **read** (worth reading when the topic comes up), **follow**
(worth seeing every post), **watch** (promising, too early), **mute** (noise or bait in this
topic), **skip** (no use for this question, nothing against it).

## A worked example (made up, numbers typical)

A long post by a developer about a year of failed side projects: 90k views, 400 likes,
380 bookmarks, 250 replies. The author's median original gets 150 views.

- Value 7/10: first-hand (2), specific about what failed and why (2), checkable only through
  the author's own story (0), new for this audience (2), useful as a case (1).
- Reach: 600x the author's median; saves almost equal likes (people keep it); replies at 0.6
  of likes (people answer the closing question).
- Flags: borrowed reach, mild (two large accounts tagged in a follow-up post); follow-back
  growth in the account (900 following, 950 followers).
- Account: three originals in recent weeks; too few to call a pattern. Verdict: **watch**.

The post is worth reading for its lessons. Its reach is a separate story: a scarce kind of post
(an honest failure), a closing question that is easy to answer, named communities that pulled
in their members, and an author who answered dozens of replies.
